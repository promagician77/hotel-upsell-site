    Reliable means no double emails, no double charges, no oversold parking.

    PMS webhooks can arrive twice and out of order; Apaleo’s own guide says a cancellation can come before the booking it cancels. Guests double-click, PMS APIs time out after doing the work, and two guests can want the last space at once. Each of these was run against the service over real HTTP.

    What the service does

    1. Checks the webhook signature, then ignores any event id it has already seen.
    2. Keeps the newest version of each reservation by timestamp; a cancellation stays final even if the booking event arrives later.
    3. Takes inventory (parking nights, late-checkout slot, upgrade room-nights) in one step before anything else, so a space is sold once.
    4. Answers the guest at once and puts folio charges, PMS room changes and CRM events in an outbox with idempotency keys.
    5. Retries after 1, 5, 15, 60 and 240 minutes, then moves the job to a dead-letter list and alerts staff. Jobs survive a restart.

    Offers go out in hotel time, never at night, and stop when they should.

    Default plan for a stay

    • Booking confirmation as soon as the booking arrives. Transactional: no unsubscribe header.
    • Offer email 7 days before arrival at 10:00 hotel time, or within 15 minutes for later bookings, unless check-in is less than 24 hours away.
    • One reminder 2 days before arrival, only if the guest neither opened the offer page nor booked.
    • Late checkout offer at 18:00 the evening before departure, only while a slot is free.
    • At most 3 offer emails per stay, none between 21:00 and 08:00, none after an unsubscribe or a cancellation.

    Checked

    10,000 random local times in Lisbon, Berlin, New York and Dubai between 2026 and 2028 convert to UTC and back exactly, including the days clocks change. On 10,000 random bookings, 0 offers fall in quiet hours and 0 arrive after check-in.

    Offer emails carry List-Unsubscribe and List-Unsubscribe-Post: List-Unsubscribe=One-Click, as Gmail asks of bulk senders’ promotional mail. Only a POST unsubscribes, so link scanners that open the link do not.

    Responses are counted from offer-page visits and bookings, not from opens: Apple Mail loads images on delivery, which makes open rates unreliable.

    How it fits with the hotel’s systems.

    A small service in the middle, with the PMS as the source of truth. Automation platforms and the CRM receive signed events, so the team can add steps without touching the core.

    Upsell service (Node.js, the engine in this demo)

    Webhook intake with signature check and de-duplication, offer rules and prices, message plans in hotel time, signed guest links, inventory counters, outbox with retries and dead-letter list, operations console.

    PMS

    Apaleo, Mews, Cloudbeds or similar: booking webhooks in; folio charges, room-type changes and availability reads out, each with an idempotency key where the PMS supports one.

    Email

    Postmark, SendGrid or Amazon SES with SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the hotel’s domain; one-click unsubscribe on offers, none on confirmations.

    Guest offer page

    Signed link that expires at checkout, shown in the guest’s language, availability checked again on open and on booking.

    CRM and automation platforms

    Signed webhooks for purchases and unsubscribes, ready for HubSpot, a hotel CRM, or a Make, Zapier or n8n scenario.

    Database

    SQLite in this demo, Postgres for a hosted version: the same one-step inventory updates and job claims.

    Operations console

    Per-guest reasons, timeline, inventory, outbox with retries, results and editable rules.

    Not measured

    • A real PMS. Webhooks follow Apaleo’s documented behaviour (repeats, any order), but no Apaleo, Mews or Cloudbeds account was used; folio charges and room changes go to a local stand-in.
    • Real email delivery: SPF, DKIM and DMARC set-up, inbox placement and spam rates. The demo writes the headers; it sends nothing.
    • Payments by card. Extras go on the guest’s bill at checkout; taking a card payment up front would add a payment provider.
    • Guest acceptance rates. The 30-day run uses random replies to test the plumbing, not to forecast revenue.
    • Load beyond one hotel, and a multi-server set-up on Postgres. The SQLite version locks with transactions; a hosted version would use the database the same way.
    • Prices, capacities and email wording, which are placeholders for the hotel to set; translations were not reviewed by native speakers.
